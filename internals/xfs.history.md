History, not rules: `internals/xfs.md` wins where they differ.

- Why XFS: reflinks; Debian's and Ubuntu's kernels have it, and it suits databases.
- User data, not a step of `setup.sh` (decided).
- The whole root, not a data partition (decided 2026-10-26, after building and testing the split): with Docker's images on the data side too, the root would hold only the system, and the root could never grow with the server. One filesystem has no size to choose, no mounts to order and nothing to fail when a partition is missing.
- Boot 1's refusals were decided on 2026-10-26, when the maintainer asked how the layout check holds up when an image changes: an initramfs from another generator would never convert, and the log would wait for it on every boot.
- Layouts found: DigitalOcean's Debian 13 has `/boot` in the root (`boot-in-root`), its Ubuntu 26.04 on a partition of its own (`boot-separate`, `vda13`); Vultr's both in the root.
- DigitalOcean wants the root as partition 1: creating a Droplet from a snapshot of a server whose root was partition 2 failed, the Droplet gone during creation (`doctl`'s 404, nothing in the panel), live or powered off, at any size; a snapshot of a stock server worked, and one with the root renumbered to 1 did (found 2026-10-26). Its own XFS image (Rocky Linux) has the root as partition 1 too.
- GRUB's BIOS core by number (`(,gpt1)`, as `grub-install` writes it): renumbering without reinstalling GRUB left a server that never booted (found 2026-10-26). The EFI config was by UUID already.
- `mv --exchange` is renameat2's `RENAME_EXCHANGE`, coreutils 9.5; without `-T` it moved the link into the directory (found).
- The last boot's script once waited for an XFS root, and ran on one with its last phase left (found).
- Ubuntu's shrunk ext4 was 3.4 GiB for 2.5 used (its inode tables). Without the move its space stayed unused before the root (tried).
- dracut: `instmods`, in `install()` or `installkernel()`, left out XFS's driver (found: `mount: unknown filesystem type 'xfs'`, the server booting on ext4 and retrying).
- Measured (2026-10-26, `s-1vcpu-512mb`, ext4 `/boot` against the XFS root on the same disk): unpacking 34,000 files (754 MB) 6.3 to 7.0 s against 7.1 to 7.8, deleting them 1.0 s against 2.5, random 16k writes 19.7k against 23.8k IOPS; a reflink copy of a 650 MB database 0.03 s. Swap on a `fallocate`d file works.
- Vultr's images (2026-10-29, `vhp-2c-2gb-amd`, 60 GB): EFI only (no BIOS boot partition, `grub-efi-amd64` alone), `/boot` in the root on both releases, the kernel's `root=UUID=` the ext4's filesystem UUID, a `/swapfile` from Vultr's vendor data (100 MB per GB of root, once per instance) and roots larger than 2 GiB. `/boot` came out at 5 and 6 GiB there; a half GiB of margin had made DigitalOcean's 3. Debian's installer leaves `# / was on /dev/vda2 during installation` in fstab, which the awk took for the root's line.
- `kho=off`: Ubuntu 26.04's kernel (7.0) has Kexec HandOver on by default (`CONFIG_KEXEC_HANDOVER_ENABLE_DEFAULT`) and marks its scratch memory CMA, for movable pages only: 626 pageblocks, 1.25 of 2 GiB on Vultr (`/proc/pagetypeinfo`; `CmaTotal` 0, `CmaFree` 830 MB once booted). In the initramfs the copy's slab, some 400 MB left, ran out: the OOM killer took `cp` and the hook, and once the kernel panicked "deadlocked on memory" and stayed there. With `kho=off` no CMA block is left (checked). Debian's 6.12 has no KHO and passes the argument to init, which ignores it. DigitalOcean's 512 MB Ubuntu converted without it (not looked into why).
- apt's timers: on Vultr's Ubuntu, unattended-upgrades (`apt-daily-upgrade.service`, some 10 s into cloud-init's final stage) upgraded systemd, which re-executed and restarted services, `cloud-init-main.service` among them, so cloud-init died in runcmd and never reached `power_state`.
- Vultr's Ubuntu runs uutils' coreutils but keeps GNU's `cp`, `mv` and `rm` (`gnucp`...); `ln`, `mkdir` and `dd` are uutils' (`dd` only on the `boot-separate` path).
- Tested on Vultr (2026-10-29): Debian 13 and Ubuntu 26.04, the conversion at 60 GB (each from the final file in one go, about two minutes), a reboot after it, `setup.sh` and `--server-info` (reflinks); no updates, release or provider snapshot.
- Tested on DigitalOcean (2026-10-26; the conversion again on both with Vultr's changes, 2026-10-29). Ubuntu 26.04 (kernel 7.0, GRUB 2.14, dracut 110): the conversion at 10 GB (8.9 GiB XFS, nothing unused), and resumed after a failure past phase 1 (the missing XFS driver); a kernel upgrade, GRUB's packages reinstalled (`grub-install` for BIOS and EFI) and a kernel removed, each with reboots; `setup.sh`; a release, its reflink snapshot and restore; a live snapshot onto a new 25 GB Droplet, a rebuild from it and a disk resize. No release upgrade (26.04 is offered none yet). Debian 13: the conversion at 10 and 25 GB; reboots; a kernel upgrade, GRUB's packages reinstalled, a kernel removed and an upgrade to forky (Debian 14: kernel 7.2, GRUB 2.14), each with reboots; `setup.sh`, a release with a database in a named volume and an owned `mnt` entry, reflink copies of both; a live snapshot onto a new 25 GB Droplet (grown, the services and data back) and a rebuild from it; a disk resize.
